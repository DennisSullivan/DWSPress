Arizona Rose

The Childhood of Lois Elaine Welling

By Lois Welling Sullivan
Edited by Teresa Prevéy and Dennis Sullivan

Arizona Rose is the childhood autobiography of Lois Elaine Welling Sullivan. Drawing on her journals, memories, family photographs, and archival materials, the book recreates life in Phoenix during the 1920s and 1930s, when citrus groves, irrigation canals, farms, and family ranches still surrounded the growing desert city.

About the Book

Through Lois's own words, readers encounter a rapidly growing desert city surrounded by citrus groves, irrigation canals, ranches, neighborhood communities, and family businesses.

The story preserves everyday experiences, family traditions, friendships, education, recreation, and the rich social history of an earlier Arizona.

Combining memoir, family history, and hundreds of archival photographs, Arizona Rose preserves a vanished Phoenix that few people today remember.

From the Book

Once upon a time in the calm and peaceful days before smog, pollution, television, computers, and World War II, Phoenix was a small town surrounded by citrus groves, small family ranches and farms.

The town itself had a frontier quality. Pima, Maricopa, and Papago Indians sat on the north sidewalks of Washington Street selling their lovely red pottery and hand woven baskets.

Historical Value

Beyond autobiography, Arizona Rose is a record of life in early twentieth-century Arizona.

The book includes family history, photographs, local stories, descriptions of businesses, schools, churches, neighborhoods, and the people who helped shape the Phoenix community.

It offers readers a firsthand account of a way of life that has largely disappeared from the modern Southwest.

Publication History

About ten years after Lois Welling Sullivan's death, her unfinished childhood autobiography was discovered among boxes of notebooks, photographs, scrapbooks, and other family materials.

Editors Teresa Prevéy and Dennis Sullivan combined Lois's memories with scrapbook entries and family photographs to preserve both her story and the visual record of her early years.

The volume concludes with My Crystal Cave, a separate essay in which Lois remembers her mother, Melda Jo Welling, and the beauty and traditions that shaped their family home.
