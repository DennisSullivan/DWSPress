William John Sullivan

William John Sullivan served as a navigator in the United States Army Air Forces during World War II. His wartime experiences eventually became the basis for Navigator, a memoir describing training, combat operations, and daily life in the South Pacific.

Navigators were responsible for guiding aircraft across vast distances using maps, charts, celestial navigation, dead reckoning, radio fixes, and careful flight planning. In the Pacific Theater, navigation errors could place crews hundreds of miles from their intended destination.

Through letters, photographs, maps, journals, and memoirs, William Sullivan preserved a remarkable record of his experiences and the people with whom he served.

Author and Archivist

Following the war, Bill Sullivan continued writing and documenting his experiences. His work included military memoirs, personal histories, essays, travel narratives, family records, and historical research.

The publication of Navigator represents the culmination of years of document preservation and family archive work. The project draws upon original source materials preserved by William Sullivan and later organized within the Sullivan Family Archive.

Legacy

DWS Press was established to preserve and publish the writings, photographs, maps, letters, memoirs, and historical records created by William John Sullivan and other members of the Sullivan family.

Current and planned publications include Navigator, Chief, Ocho, and other works drawn from the family archive.

Through these publications, the goal is to preserve authentic voices and firsthand historical experiences for future readers, researchers, descendants, and historians.
