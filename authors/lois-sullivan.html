Lois Welling Sullivan

Lois Welling Sullivan was a writer, artist, correspondent, autobiographer, traveler, historian of family life, and one of the principal creative voices preserved within the Sullivan Family Archive.

Through letters, journals, personal reflections, artwork, and published works, she documented the people, places, and experiences that shaped her life and the life of her family.

Writer

Lois wrote extensively throughout her life. Her surviving writings include autobiographical accounts, personal correspondence, travel narratives, journals, family history materials, and reflections on everyday life.

Her writing combines careful observation, humor, honesty, and an appreciation for both family history and personal experience.

Artist

In addition to her writing, Lois created artwork and watercolor paintings that documented people, places, memories, and experiences important to her life.

These artistic works remain an important part of the Sullivan Family Archive and serve as source material for current and future DWS Press projects.

Travel and Ocho

Lois played a central role in documenting the family's six-month journey through Mexico in 1958.

Her letters, observations, and firsthand accounts provide some of the most important source material for the developing publication project Ocho.

The surviving correspondence from that journey preserves a unique record of family travel, Mexican culture, and mid-century American life.

Publications

  • Arizona Rose — autobiography
  • Conjugating Love — writings and artwork
  • Ocho — travel narrative developed with William John Sullivan

Legacy

Lois preserved letters, manuscripts, artwork, photographs, family records, and personal writings that continue to enrich the Sullivan Family Archive.

Her work continues to contribute to the mission of DWS Press and to the preservation of the family's historical record.
