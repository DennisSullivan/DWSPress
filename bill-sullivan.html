William John Sullivan

William John Sullivan was a World War II Army Air Forces navigator, memoirist, traveler, family historian, and lifelong observer of the world around him.

His writings document military service, family life, travel, work, and personal experiences across much of the twentieth century.

World War II Service

During World War II, Bill served as a navigator in the United States Army Air Forces. His wartime experiences took him from training fields in the United States to operational service in the South Pacific.

Those experiences would later become the foundation for Navigator, his memoir of training, combat operations, navigation, and military life.

Throughout his life he preserved maps, photographs, records, and memoir materials that now form an important part of the Sullivan Family Archive.

Author

Bill wrote extensively about his experiences, preserving stories that might otherwise have been lost with time.

His memoirs and writings combine personal recollections, historical documentation, and firsthand observations of the people and events that shaped his life.

His published and archival works include:

  • Navigator — World War II memoir
  • Chief — autobiographical writings and recollections
  • Ocho — family travel narrative developed with Lois Sullivan

The Archive

Bill's surviving papers include memoirs, correspondence, photographs, maps, military records, travel narratives, and family history materials.

These materials serve as primary source documents for DWS Press publications and for the continuing preservation of the Sullivan Family Archive.
