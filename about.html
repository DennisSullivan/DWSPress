About DWS Press

DWS Press was established to preserve and publish the writings, artwork, photographs, maps, letters, memoirs, journals, and historical records of the Sullivan Family Archive.

What began as a family effort to preserve important documents and photographs has evolved into a publishing program dedicated to making these materials available to future readers, researchers, historians, and descendants.

The archive centers on the lives and creative works of William John Sullivan and Lois Welling Sullivan, whose writings, photographs, artwork, correspondence, memoirs, and family records span much of the twentieth century.

The Sullivan Family Archive

The archive contains military memoirs, travel narratives, autobiographical writings, family histories, photographs, maps, correspondence, journals, artwork, and other historical materials preserved over multiple generations.

These collections document military service, family life, travel, artistic expression, and everyday experiences that might otherwise have been lost with time.

The archive continues to grow as additional writings, photographs, and family records are identified, organized, digitized, and prepared for publication.

William John Sullivan

William John Sullivan served as a navigator in the United States Army Air Forces during World War II. Throughout his life he preserved memoirs, mission records, photographs, maps, letters, and personal writings documenting both his wartime experiences and his life afterward.

His published and archival works include Navigator, Chief, and the developing publication project Ocho.

Lois Welling Sullivan

Lois Welling Sullivan contributed letters, autobiographical writings, artwork, watercolors, family records, and personal reflections that form a significant part of the archive.

Her published works include Arizona Rose, her autobiography, and Conjugating Love, a collection built from her writings and artwork.

Her letters and travel writings also provide essential source material for the continuing development of Ocho.

Our Purpose

DWS Press exists to preserve authentic voices and firsthand experiences through publication.

By transforming family archives into published books, we seek to ensure that personal histories become part of the historical record and remain accessible to future generations.
