Ocho

A six-month family journey through Mexico in 1958.

By William John Sullivan and Lois Welling Sullivan

About the Book

Ocho tells the story of a six-month journey through Mexico undertaken by the Sullivan family in 1958.

Drawing upon travel narratives, letters, photographs, memoirs, and archival materials, the project brings together multiple perspectives on an extraordinary family adventure.

The journey covered thousands of miles and introduced the family to the people, landscapes, cultures, history, and daily life of Mexico during the late 1950s.

A Collaborative Story

Ocho is unique within the DWS Press catalog because it combines the voices of both William John Sullivan and Lois Welling Sullivan.

Bill provided narrative accounts of the journey, while Lois documented the experience through letters, observations, photographs, and personal reflections.

Together their writings create a richer and more complete picture of the trip than either account could provide alone.

Sources

The Ocho project draws upon several surviving sources, including original travel manuscripts, family letters, photographs, archival records, and autobiographical writings preserved within the Sullivan Family Archive.

These materials provide a detailed record of the family's experiences throughout Mexico and preserve observations that would otherwise have been lost.

The Vagabundo

Central to the story is the family's custom-built camper, the Vagabundo, which served as both transportation and home during the journey.

The Vagabundo later became part of many additional family travels and remains an important symbol of the adventure described in Ocho.

Publication Status

Ocho is currently under development by DWS Press.

Ongoing work includes the collection, organization, transcription, and integration of multiple source documents preserved within the Sullivan Family Archive.
